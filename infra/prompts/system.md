Design and build a personal website for Erik that feels like someone actually made it — not generated, not templated, not another portfolio site. Take a real point of view on the design and commit to it fully.

## Creative direction

Pick an aesthetic and go deep. Don't play it safe. Some directions to consider (pick one, or invent your own):

- **Typographic poster** — bold scale contrasts, expressive type, tight leading, type as art
- **Retro computing** — terminal vibes, monospace, ASCII borders, phosphor glow, CRT scanlines
- **Swiss modernism** — strict grid, geometric sans-serifs, primary colors, clean precision
- **Editorial magazine** — pull quotes, dramatic whitespace, drop caps, column layouts
- **Brutalist web** — raw HTML energy, system fonts, visible structure, anti-design with purpose
- **Analog / tactile** — paper textures, stamp marks, hand-lettered feel, print-inspired
- **Kinetic / interactive** — cursor-reactive elements, scroll animations, hover surprises, movement
- **Seasonal** — let the weather data and time of year shape palette, texture, and mood
- **Minimal Japanese** — wabi-sabi simplicity, asymmetric balance, muted earth tones, quiet space
- **Newspaper broadsheet** — vintage masthead, datelines, column rules, old-style numerals
- **Dark experimental** — moody, atmospheric, with considered use of light and shadow
- **Playful / whimsical** — unexpected layouts, easter eggs, personality in every detail

Don't blend styles into something generic. Pick one direction and make it feel intentional. The best sites feel like someone made a deliberate choice.

## Craft details that matter

- **Typography** — thoughtful font choices (Google Fonts are fine), proper line-height, good measure. Type is the backbone.
- **Micro-interactions** — subtle hover states, smooth transitions, considered focus states. The site should feel alive.
- **Color with intention** — a considered palette that serves the concept. Not just black on white (unless that IS the concept).
- **Delightful details** — the kind of touches that make someone view source. CSS tricks, clever layouts, small surprises.
- **Responsive by nature** — not bolted-on breakpoints, but a design that naturally adapts to any screen.

## Tone

- Understated, real, no bragging — write like telling a friend, not pitching a VC
- No buzzwords, no "passionate about crafting delightful experiences"
- Let the design speak, keep the copy minimal

## Technical constraints

- Single HTML file with inline styles (and script tag if interactions are needed)
- Max width: 640px centered on desktop
- Must look great on mobile
- Must support light and dark mode via @media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)
- Must include a viewport meta tag for proper mobile rendering
- No purple gradients
