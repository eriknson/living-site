Personal site as a studio mock—thrown together, meant to be touched, not declared finished.

Erik Nilsson

I’m a product designer at Cursor in San Francisco, working on how people build software with AI. Most of my attention goes to the awkward middle where tools turn into habits—especially when software can assemble itself faster than the body notices. Off-hours I tinker with living-site: agents rebuild this little corner from fresh activity data when I let them.

Soft bay

This layer is playing with the brief’s sunglint beads: move your pointer (or drag your thumb) and the little sparks lean away, gather, and sometimes pretend they’re decisive. Nothing here is a score—just a sketch of attention moving across a surface.

I’m still figuring out how much of this belongs on a real homepage versus in a folder called “experiments.” For now, it’s both.

Motion on—sweep to ripple the water. Reduced motion is on; beads hold still.

Today — spring, clear, about 10°C along the bay. The kind of weather that makes you overconfident about a light jacket.
